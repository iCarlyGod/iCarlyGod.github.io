Hey all, recently, I've returned to the old competitive games I used to play like I would to an abusive spouse.  I go to my university's Melee weekly and I've started coaching a couple of teams in Splatoon.  Funny enough, right when I come back to Splatoon, a major controversy rose from the ashes: top players being racist and queerphobic. (I hope that's the right term.  I checked one Reddit thread asking about the use of the term and it said it was okay so, if I get cancelled for this later, my bad.  I did the minimum research for this, and I didn't think about it too deeply about it.)  

A lot of the controversy is about the derogatory remarks, but also the fact that other top players and community members that knew about it stayed silent about it all.  People were saying it was equivalent to the "2020 Smash community purge" which I can understand in the sense it is top players misusing their platform and setting bad examples, but as some who followed both Smash and Splatoon extremely closely, this recent controvery was definitely much smaller in scale to the one four years ago.  

Derogatory remarks, while distasteful, are, at worst, a less severe crime than what certain stories revealed four years ago.  That's not to say that these players should get away scot-free, but I think people try too hard to compare things to each other when it doesn't do anything to help the situation.  I think the players in question, that I will leave the names out of because it doesn't help the situation, deserve to have action taken against them, but the severity of the matter is up to the Splatoon Safety... group (I forgot what it's called oops.)  


Another thing about this controversy are the "Nintendo was right for never supporting the competitive scene" posts on social media.  Personally, these posts are misguided, destructive to the competitive communities, and have things backwards.  From my experience, people see situations like these without actually being in the community and let the rotten apples spoil the bunch.  It happened four years ago with the Smash community, and now people are letting it happen again but to the Splatoon community.  The other thing is that if Nintendo was more involved and the community didn't have to rely on individuals to step up in the community to invoke competition, this wouldn't be happening in the first place.  Nintendo, the supposed Disney-equivalent in the gaming industry, would be way more dilligent, strict, and attentive when it comes to handling and controlling behavior of competitors at their own tournaments.  

For context, Riot Games was fining the players on the best Valorant team in the world for trash talking last year, and it's not like they were saying anything derogatory.  I think at most, they dropped a couple of swear words, but this is in the competitive scene of a game with realistic guns, voice lines with swearing, blood splatter effects, and death.  If Riot would go to such lengths to maintain a clean image, wouldn't it be reasonable to assume Nintendo goes to the same, if not greater, lengths to maintain their clean, family-friendly image?  

Like it's so fucking aggrivating shit like this happens and people then make the conclusion "Oh! Nintendo knew about this and didn't want to associate want to associate their family-friendly brand with these disgusting freaks."  People don't see racially charged terrorist attacks in another country and go "Oh! The government of my home country knew about this deep-rooted racism in this other country and now I understand that they were right."  Like, is it *that weird* to think that the freakishly squeaky clean corporation would freakishly ensure a squeaky clean PR in their competitions?  At the very least, wouldn't the unsupervised, unaddressed immaturity be the result of a lack of need of maturity in a community surrounded around a game designed to be played by children? 

Nintendo getting involved would be a good thing for the community.  I don't mean to say they should completely control the competitive community because the grassroots aspect allows people to be loose and feel comfortable, and that is why the Smash scene thrives being built in grassroots for 20+ years.  I just think if Nintendo had their own events that were in line with the competitive scene's interests and values, and still allowed grassroots and independent TOs to run their own events, that would be an ideal state of the community to be in.  I think if the community always looks upon Nintendo with skepticism and cynicism, these sort of controversies and community identities will never the leave the Smash or Splatoon community.  


The last thing I want to talk about is how people wanted to have rules and the code of conduct within the community to change in response to the situation.  Personally, I hate rules.  They lack adjustments to nuanced circumstances or edge cases, which constantly leads to rulebooking and loopholing.  When it comes to derogatory remarks, there can't be a fine line of policing conversations to ensure people in the community are saying unproblematic things and allowing people to hold private conversations regarding competition.  You can't control all conversations while simulataneous giving people their privacy in their conversations; it's paradoxal.   

It's also worth mentioning how it's hard to address this sort of thing. There are many reasons this is hard:
1. People get accused of making a mountain out of a molehill when they identify a potential flag for problematic behavior
2. The people that make fun of the people that try to shine a light on things muddy the waters on the actual issue, which directs the controversy away from the original flag and into the "is the world too soft" controvesies.
3. People have some subconcious sense of this truth and feel it is not worth the social media scrutiny to expose the behavior
4. Those are resilient enough to go through the cycle above usually get labelled as "attention seekers", "drama starters", and "overly negative", which most people don't like hearing about themselves and don't want to be around those sort of people too.
5. Realistically speaking, there are so many social nuances in modern humor, especially with teenagers, that it's hard to completely distingush cases of "this is problematic behavior" from "this is just a layered inside joke with several mutual understandings I don't quite get."

That fifth point is not necessarily problematic behavior.  The difference is that derogatory remarks are both used to dehumanize people and as a form of edgy humor between two people that mutually find it to be a point of humor.  I don't think I need to explain that dehumanizing groups of people is bad and makes people feel unsafe.  However, I, as a very un-asian asian in North America, have seen, heard of, and had many cases of mutual racism in healthy friend groups.  It's not like cases where it's a group of white people making racist remarks towards black people and using dehumanizing terminology, it would (usually be a friend group solely comprised of PoC) making remarks that dehumanize themselves based on their race which progresses to a verbal snowball fight of edgy, but playful derogatory remarks towards each other.  I get the latter isn't near the significant majority of these derogatory remarks, but it's important to realize things aren't as simple as banning derogatory statements for being derogatory, because it's also not unusual for mutual exchanges of derogatory remarks to be a pillar in a friendship in the modern day.  As far as I'm aware, normal people are able to control themselves to only make derogatory statements towards themselves and towards friends that are close enough to joke about that sort of thing. 

These nuances in the behavioral patterns on current-day youth and young adults is why I can't really see there being a change in the code of conduct in response to this situation.  Rules suck because they aren't being made to stop the people they should be addressed to: rulebookers and loophole abusers.  Those that only technically break rules by circumstance are left out to dry by the imperfections of the rules and those that are slimy enough to try and cheat out the rules rob the common people blind.  In this case, changing the code of conduct to be more harsh on derogatory statements and remarks would only lead to rulebooking and making problems out of nothing.  As someone who used to be overly stiff about things, (and is called stiff from time to time still,) people do not want to be friends with people that are overly stiff about abiding rules.  No one wants to be in a community where they constantly need to watch what they say in fear of not properly abiding by the rules.


To close off this post, I just want this situation to be handled properly and I want less social media traction around the controversy.  In the world of competitive gaming, this is not that serious and the community shouldn't make it out to be.  With that being said, the situation still deserves to be taken seriously, but just not in the way it is being paraded and inflated in the severity it is currently being addressed by the wider, immature hivemind of the Splatoon community.  I don't think some derogatory remarks made at a fairly young age should be a social death sentence, but definitely deserve at least a year or so given by the evidence already out there.  

That's all I got to say on that.  Please just watch where and when to say certain things, what things to say to who, and why and how you say it.  People, especially online, are out for blood, and until you learn to just say nothing at all, be wary.  Have a good one.
