Playing randomly is what everyone thinks they have to do in order to compete at a high level in a competitive video game.  Competitive video games are filled with small, quick interactions that can imitate various simple simulataneous games.  It's common in video games to talk about a "RPS" dynamic or "mix-ups" or other concepts that involve guessing what the opponent will do in the small amount of time they have to decide.  I don't know if this is true for others, but I used to think I had to be random in order to be successful because of these interactions involving both players making simple guesses.  Now that I've taken a look into inner psychology of these games, I've realized that not only does trying to be super random make for really inefficient use of mental energy, but it's also impossible for a human to make truly random decisions in these circumstances.  Humans aren't random and it's hard to be random while also mentally juggling a real-time competitive video game.

So the first question I should answer is "If I'm not supposed to play randomly, how am I suppose to play?"  The way you are supposed to play these micro-interactions is by picking an option you think will beat what your opponent will do.  I get it sounds like I'm saying "if your opponent picks rock, pick paper," but I'm not.  What I'm suggesting is that you use your prior interactions with your opponent to gauge what options they will pick in the current interaction.  There is always at least a small bit of information you can work with to gauge what your opponent will do.  Take the passage below as an example of the types of information you can gather for something as simple as rock-paper-scissors.

```
"My opponent is a very skilled player.  They look like they tend to pick paper given how tense their fist is, but I think this is a trick they do in order to bait me to pick scissors.  I think I should pick paper, because I'll win if I'm right about the bait and I'll at least draw if my read on their tendency to pick paper is correct."  
```

Now there is natural randomness to this idea.  For example, if I asked you what your favourite fruit was, you may not give an answer instantaneously.  If you instantly came up with an answer, I would guess you are a very simple or obsessive person.  If not, you might have internally come up with a list of fruits you often see and eat, and narrow the list down from there.  Now what's important to understand about this internal list of fruits is that it had about like five fruits on it initially and the list differs from person to person.  

Now, what if I asked what you favourite fruit was that isn't a durian?  You may just think "well, my favourite fruit isn't durian, so I'll just say my favourite fruit."  To the poor durian lovers, I'm sorry for making you think up another fruit.  My point is that there are situations that are similar enough to respond in the same way, and as long as the added nuances of the situation don't make the option selected more problematic.

Now what do you think your opponent's favourite fruit is?  Most likely, you answered with "I don't know.  How am I supposed to know?" or with something along the lines of "Apples... maybe????" The point is that you have to have preconceptions about your opponent in order to predict them.  Everyone eats apples, so apples is a pretty safe pick overall.  Maybe you know you know your opponent is weird and likes eating lemons... for some reason.  Maybe you think everyone likes strawberries so your opponent also likes strawberries.  

Now to bring this back to video games, you have to have preconceptions on your opponent in order to predict what they will do.  You have to use subtle clues in their gameplay to figure out what they will choose to do.  Now just like there are people that love eating raw pumpkin, there are people who are going to have weird impulses or have completely bizarre reasoning for the bizarre preconceptions.  These extremely small minority are the type of people that tend to be seen as being unpredicatable.  The wires in their brain are all mixed up and it's difficult to understand how to figure out what they are doing.  

However, from what I've come to understand, it's very hard to consistently be unpredictable and skilled at a game.  When your logical reasoning is flawed, your ability to correctly adapt comes into question.  Usually, the people that are hard to get a read on are skilled at understanding what other people think about.  It's also hard to get a read on people the harder you think about it.  A lot more energy goes into brute forcing certain ideas, which is kind of like finding something you've lost the moment you give up.  

To conclude, the best way to get a read on your opponent is to find a position where you can relax a little.  Tension, nerves, pressure, restraint, and anything else that requires manual thinking will compromise brain power you can put towards evaluating the current situation.  It also helps in competition to minimize excess use of mental energy to minimize fatigue over the course of the competition.  Relaxing also helps with seemingly playing random.  Maybe you play towards your emotions or your personality a little and pick an option that has more risk, but beat the option you are sure the opponent will choose.  People don't play randomly.  They are aware of their situation and just do things that exceeed what others expect them to do.
